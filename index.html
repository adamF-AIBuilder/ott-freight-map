How the Freight Map works

Zillow, but the "homes" are the lanes, shippers and delivery stops that already put freight on OTT trucks.

What you're looking at

The three views

  • Shippers — accounts first (MSC, Nestle, Siemens rolled up by name), sites underneath. Opens here. "Steady" = 30+ loads and active 6 of 8 months: that is the call list.
  • Where the money is — every truck ton coming into the NY metro by origin region (federal FAF data), with our share as the purple dot inside. Turn on shipper density to see where manufacturers and wholesalers sit. Lanes show the market price per shipment and how many shippers in the ZIP area we have never touched.
  • Lanes — each line is a corridor: an origin area (ZIP-3) to the NY region where its freight lands. Thicker = more dollars. The top lanes follow the real highways. Click a lane for its shippers, loads per week and road miles.
  • Delivery stops — the addresses where it lands. Who we deliver to.

Reading the map

  • Zoomed out, shippers and stops group into bubbles with total $ and loads. Click a bubble: it zooms in and lists the top names inside it. Zoom in and every site gets its own price pin.
  • NEW = first load was July or later.
  • The list on the right always shows what's on the map, ranked by $. Pan or zoom and it updates; the header totals the view.
  • Filters: carrier, months, weight, cadence (steady / new), hazmat. Search by company, account, city, ZIP or PRO. Delivery stops hide one-off addresses by default (21,595 of 29,382 addresses saw one load; mostly residential).
  • Billed to us is our last-mile slice, not the prize. Next to it: ton-miles/wk (size of the whole move) and, per lane, trucks/wk and the rough cost to haul it ourselves. The dollars the national carrier bills the shipper are not in this data; the portal's carrier rate APIs can price any lane on request.

Click anything

  • You get its page: $ by month, carrier mix, who ships there / where it goes, what the freight is, every load.
  • Its lanes draw in blue along the highways; everything else fades. Amber glow = other shipper sites within 20 road-miles of those lanes: freight one OTT truck could pick up on the same run.

Interline, in one breath

Today a national carrier hauls the freight from, say, Pennsylvania to its own terminal and OTT does the last leg and bills them for it. Two carriers, one shipment. The $ here is OTT's slice. The play is to run the long leg ourselves, which is why "which lane" and "who is on it" are the questions this map answers.

Get something out of it

  • ★ Targets — star any lane, shipper or stop; the Targets list has a CSV for sales.
  • Export view — CSV of what's on the map right now.
  • Share link — opens straight to that page.
